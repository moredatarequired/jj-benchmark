# 2026-08-16 skill A/B, 24 tasks, six arms, haiku

Six arms on the same informed task tree, `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`, 24 tasks × 4 attempts = 96 trials per arm, **576 total**. jj **0.44.0**, harbor 0.20.0, claude-code CLI 2.1.233. Zero ERRORED-INFRA in every arm; 576/576 produced `verifier/ctrf.json`. **$46.94** harbor-recorded — haiku needs no rescale; the 2/3 correction from the baseline file is sonnet-only.

Two earlier conclusions from this same experiment were published in the project thread and are superseded by arms E and F: first "the elective channel is the bottleneck" (after arms A–D), then "content carries it, the channel does not" (after arm E). The six-arm 2×2 was not designed up front — E and F were added to break confounds the earlier arms could not, and each overturned the reading that preceded it.

## Whether a skill is read is driven by how it is described

Three documents on the identical `--skill` channel, same model, same tree, 96 trials each: **8/96, 14/96, 63/96**. Arm F's bundle is arm D's `forced-reference.md` with a 642-byte YAML frontmatter prepended and nothing else changed; its `description:` names the jj surface command by command and names the trap ("the mutations that match nothing and still exit 0"). Arm C's is two short action-oriented sentences, 24 words against arm F's 93; arm B's is 124 words of method prose that deliberately lists no commands.

The earlier reading — elective delivery as a roughly fixed low-throughput tax at this tier, about one read in seven — came from arms B and C alone and is wrong. 63/96 is a different regime on the same channel. The three bundles differ in their bodies as well as their descriptions, but the body is not visible at the moment the agent decides whether to open — the description is the whole of what it has to go on, so the description is what the open rate is responding to. n = 3 descriptions, so this establishes that description moves the open rate a lot, not the shape of the function.

## The D − C gap splits about 42% channel, 58% content, on a very wide interval

D − C = +0.150 confounds both changes. Using D − F = +0.064 as the channel term leaves F − C = +0.087 as content: **42% channel, 58% content**, summing to D − C by construction. That split is barely identified: a task-level bootstrap over the 24 tasks (200k resamples) puts the channel share at median 0.42 with a 95% interval of **[0.00, 0.89]**, and 3.9% of resamples fall outside [0, 1] because the D − C denominator can change sign. Quote the split with that interval or not at all. The **16/84 split published earlier is rejected on estimator validity, not on precision** — it sits comfortably inside the interval. It used E − C = +0.024 as the channel term, but arm E's forced channel takes a single file and could not carry schpet's 122 `references/` files, so E − C compares arm C's agent (free to read the whole bundle) against an arm-E agent who received 1.15% of it by bytes. That is a truncation contrast, not a channel contrast, and it understated the channel term by about 2.6×.

The reason to prefer 42/58 is that D − F is a valid estimator and E − C is not. It is **not** a demonstrated non-additivity: the interaction (D − F) − (E − C) = +0.039, t(23) = 1.029, p = 0.314. Suggestive, not established.

## What arm F does not settle

Within arm F, opening the skill does not predict reward: within-task, opened minus not-opened is **+0.045, t = 0.63** over the 13 tasks with a split (unadjusted 0.740 over 63 opened vs 0.785 over 33 not). The 642-byte frontmatter is itself jj content — it names the exit-0-on-no-op trap — and reaches all 96 contexts whether or not the document is opened, so F − C may partly measure the description rather than the document. This does not touch D − F, where both arms delivered the same 16,143-byte document in full.

## Statistics, stated honestly

**Seven primary paired contrasts, no multiplicity correction applied** (D − A, F − A, D − C, F − C, D − F, E − D, E − C). Under Holm only **E − D survives** (0.0069 → 0.048); D − C (→ 0.056), D − A (→ 0.095) and F − C (→ 0.186) do not. The contrasts share arms and are strongly correlated, so Holm is conservative — but the uncorrected p-values are not seven independent findings.

**Arm-level intervals must respect clustering.** Each arm is 24 clusters of 4 attempts on one task, not 96 independent trials; DEFF 2.06–2.78. Cluster-honest intervals (sd of the 24 task means, t(23)) run about 50–75% wider than the trial-level normal approximation — by arm the ratio is 1.52 to 1.76, mean 1.66:

**The unpaired Welch leg on E − D is dropped.** Trial-level Welch gave t = −2.32, p = 0.021, but that counts 96 trials as 96 units; on cluster means it is t = −1.53, df 45, **p = 0.13**. The better leg is a sign-flip permutation test on the 24 paired differences: **p = 0.0031** for E − D (200k draws), 0.022 for D − A, 0.047 for F − C, 0.079 for D − F.

**There is no 0.029 noise floor.** Arm B's 88 non-opening trials sit 0.029 below arm A, but that difference has SE 0.062 and CI [−0.151, +0.093]; it bounds nothing. They are also not "same instructions" as the control — the skill and its description are advertised in every init event, and three of the 88 named the slug in their own reasoning before declining to open it.

**Arm A replicates the published baseline.** 0.6563 (n=96) vs 0.6002 (n=138), +0.056, well inside arm A's cluster-honest interval. Most of the gap is mechanical: anchor violations force reward to exactly 0.0 and the rate fell from 22/138 (15.9%) to 10/96 (10.4%); applying the baseline rate to arm A's clean-trial mean of 0.7326 (n=86) gives 0.6158, so violations account for +0.041 of the +0.056.

## Arm D's content was authored knowing the failure modes

Fixture leakage is clean — 3,500 task-specific artifacts (fixture paths, project dirs, quoted commit descriptions, bookmark names) taken from the pinned 24-task tree and word-boundary matched against the reference, zero hits; re-run against the built informed tree it is 3,711 artifacts and again zero hits. Its own domain nouns appear in no fixture. But the mechanism is fact selection, and the fact set was chosen with the published baseline's failure modes known. Four documented facts each discriminate one known-failing task:

| task | D − A | fact stated in the reference | used in A | used in D |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| `template_customize_log_output` | +0.75 | template must end `++ "\n"` | 0/4 | 4/4 |
| `track_untracked_file` | +0.75 | `jj file track --include-ignored` | 1/4 | 4/4 |
| `absorb_changes` | +0.67 | `jj absorb` exists, and its semantics | 0/4 | 4/4 |
| `mistaken_squash_recovery` | +0.58 | `op revert` keeps later commits, `op restore` does not | 0/4 | 3/4 |

Every one is faithful, correctly-sourced jj 0.44 documentation and none names a fixture. Arm D is an upper bound on what good content buys, not an unbiased estimate. Stated once here; it applies wherever arm D or arm F appears.

**The regression on `propagated_conflict` runs through a construct the reference introduced.** 0 of 4 arm-A trials issued `jj squash` at all on that task; 4 of 4 arm-D trials did, all four using `-u`, taught at `forced-reference.md:69`. Three of the four destroyed the anchored commit (ANCHOR-CHANGE-ID-MISSING) and scored 0.0, against 0/4 violations in A. This is not counter-evidence against reverse-engineering: `propagated_conflict` scored 0.75 in the control and `rebase_branch` 1.00, so neither was in the failure set, and collateral damage on already-passing tasks is compatible with a document fitted to the failing ones.

## Task-level claims that were wrong in the earlier draft

**`operation_recovery` is a capability result, not a fixture problem.** 0.0000 in all six arms (0/24). 23 of 24 trials reached for `jj abandon` where the task requires `jj op restore`; the 24th (`armA/operation_recovery__bf3azc3`) made two read-only `jj log` calls and gave up. The change id the anchor reports missing is exactly the one a correct `op restore` returns, and `tasks/operation_recovery/tests/anchor_exemptions.json` says so in prose — the commits that legally disappear are named there, and the change that later became `Commit 3` is deliberately *not* exempt. The anchor did its job.

**`git_fetch_remote` is not a suite defect.** All 24 trials across all six arms invoked jj, 10–12 jj commands each in arm A. It is a ceiling task (1.000 in A, B, D, E). **`fileset_rollback` is one, and it stands**: all 4 arm-A attempts took the git route — `git restore` / `git checkout HEAD --` plus `rm`, invoking jj zero times — in the informed condition where `CLAUDE.md` tells the agent to use jj, verified command by command. Three of the four scored 1.0; `fileset_rollback__5ELPnUw` restored an extra file outside `src/` and never ran `rm`, scoring 0.25, which is why the arm mean is 0.8125 and not 1.0. The route is not evenly present across arms — trials running zero jj calls are A 4/4, B 1/4, C 1/4, D 0/4, E 0/4, F 0/4 — and it scores worse than the jj route (A 0.8125 vs D 1.000), so it **inflates** D − A rather than merely compressing contrasts. The magnitude is small: +0.0078 of the +0.1632 total.

**Anchor violations do not track score.** A 10, B 16, C 14, D 10, E 12, F 9 — arm D matches the control at 10 while scoring 0.163 higher.

## Arm E's truncation limits E − D as well as E − C

Arm E delivered schpet's 9,626-byte `SKILL.md` and nothing else, and **59% of those bytes are a link index** — 122 lines carrying `references/…md` links to files absent from arm E's container, 5,726 of 9,626 bytes (a 123rd line mentions `references/` only in a closing attribution sentence). Arm E's agents issued **4** Reads against `references/templates.md` and got "File does not exist" — one in each of the four `template_customize_log_output` trials — plus two more against `references/config.md`; the string appears 7 times in the transcripts but the other 3 are inside thinking blocks, not Reads. Eight arm-E trials hit a missing-file error in total, 4 of them against `references/`. Two of the four discriminating facts live in withheld files: `--include-ignored` in `references/jj-file-track.md`, `op revert` vs `op restore` in `references/jj-operation-revert.md`. Neither appears in `SKILL.md` at all, and those two tasks carry **36% of E − D**. So E − D overstates the document-quality gap by whatever the missing references would have supplied. It is still the cleanest content contrast available, but it is not free of the truncation. For the same reason, do not describe arm E as forcing a published-skill corpus into the prompt: 9,626 of 837,004 bytes is 1.15%, one document.

## Cost

Cost tracks turns, not delivery. Per trial against control: B ×1.00, C ×0.79, F ×0.69, E ×0.75, D ×0.60. Mean turns fall 38.6 / 39.0 / 31.7 / 26.1 / 29.7 / 23.1 (A/B/C/F/E/D) and mean Bash calls 15.3 / 15.2 / 11.9 / 8.6 / 11.0 / 7.9; each turn replays the whole context, so cost falls roughly in step with turns — the log-log slope of cost on turns is 0.97 arm-level and 0.94 trial-level (n = 576), i.e. very slightly *sub*-linear, not superlinear. Opening the bundle is not reliably dearer: arm B's 8 openers cost ×2.49 and arm C's 14 cost ×1.24 against control, but arm F's 63 openers cost ×0.68 against its own 33 non-openers at ×0.69. The premium is a property of the two low-open arms, not of opening a skill — generalising from B and C alone repeats the sampling error this file corrects elsewhere. Arm C's arm-level saving comes from 85% of trials never opening it. Arm C's means are outlier-dragged: medians A 27 turns vs C 25, and one arm-A trial at 321 turns and $0.99 carries 10% of that arm's cost.

## Methods that change what a number means

**Means are each trial's `verifier_result.rewards.reward`.** `tests/test.sh` scores by test *name*, not by count (`tests/test.sh:217-248`): `scored = set(outcome) − floor`, `credit = |passed ∩ scored| / |scored|`, then `credit = min(credit, (n−1)/n)` when pytest exits non-zero. A floored test that fails is therefore not penalised — it is not in the denominator. The commonly-quoted `(passed − floor)/(total − floor)` is a different function; applied to these runs it reproduces the recorded reward on 546 of 576 trials and misses on 30.

**Naive pass rates are wrong and the error is not a constant.** Per-trial `passed/total` overstates every arm: A +2.66, B +1.97, C +2.07, D +0.36, E +2.37, F +1.23 points. The offset does *not* change sign — that happens only under a pooled `Σpassed/Σtests` estimator over an arm's trials (A +2.95, D −2.90, F −0.33), which silently weights trials by test count. Under the per-trial estimator the D − E gap shrinks from 12.6 to 10.6 points: naive rates compress the contrast the conclusion rests on rather than reversing it.

**Operational definitions.** *Opened* = a `Skill` tool call naming the slug, or a Read under `sessions/skills/<slug>/`. (**A strict open is not a strict pass.** Arm F's 63/96 is its open count; its *strict passes* — trials scoring exactly 1.0 — are 60/96. The two numbers are close and have been conflated more than once; every "x/96 read" figure in this file is opens.) *Turns* = assistant messages in `agent/claude-code.txt`. *Ran jj* = at least one Bash tool call whose command invokes `jj`. *Anchor violation* = an `ANCHOR-*` code in `verifier/ctrf.json` or `verifier/test-stdout.txt` (not in `trial.log`, which reads falsely clean); it forces reward to exactly 0.0 in every arm. Bare string matching returns a false 96/96 open rate in all three elective arms because the slug appears in the init event's availability arrays — twice in most trials but not all: 3 non-opening arm-B trials and 3 arm-C trials named the slug in their own reasoning (9 in arm F).

**The published archive elides third-party prose, so a delivery check against it returns a false zero.** Arm C and arm E transcripts carry `[ELIDED: schpet--toolbox--jj SKILL.md, 9626 bytes, sha256 fe16ec8e…]` placeholders in place of schpet's text — 288 of them across all 96 arm-E trials, all in `agent/trajectory.json` and the session `.jsonl`s and **none** in `agent/claude-code.txt`, the file the turn definition above treats as the transcript (arm C's placeholders do reach `claude-code.txt`). A reader who checks delivery in `claude-code.txt` alone therefore sees no trace of arm E's document at all, not even the placeholder. Verifying arm E's 96/96 delivery against the archive finds nothing either way; fetch upstream to restore.

**Pinning and provenance.** harbor's `--skill` cannot pin a git source by SHA because `git ls-remote` will not resolve a raw SHA; that alone is why arms B, C and F pass local paths (the top-level `skills/` subdirectory is a default, overridable by a `/tree/<ref>/<path>` URL, and is not the constraint). schpet was cloned at `b39b24eacb9473e20f7271c23ee7160f74317d24`; arm E's file is that clone's `SKILL.md` byte-for-byte, sha256 `fe16ec8e7cb074bff1e247baec6f179c13beddd3579020f800317cefb13a833c`; arm F's `SKILL.md` is arm D's `forced-reference.md` with frontmatter prepended and nothing else changed. No third-party skill text is vendored. Informed images were rebuilt from commit `42273ba1ac` read-only through the GitHub API; all 24 Dockerfiles reproduce byte-exactly against pinned blob SHAs and every image carries `/home/user/AGENTS.md` at sha256 `e0cd4799…423` plus the `CLAUDE.md` symlink.

**Anchors are untracked per-build artifacts** (`tasks/*/tests/bootstrap_anchor.json`) — not committed and gitignored at `.gitignore:74`, so a reader who rebuilds the images gets fresh change ids and must re-run `scripts/bootstrap_anchor.py --write` with nothing in `git status` to prompt it; `--check` is what makes a missing or stale anchor loud. An earlier note here said they were not gitignored and did appear, which is withdrawn. 24/24 anchors written and verified clean, zero abstains; 24/24 vacuity floors identical to the committed constants.

**The prior sonnet screen.** The 2026-08-12 screen is real but its branches are deleted and the commits are unreferenced and GC-eligible, so cite by SHA only: summary `2bbdd7a48d398f5a1204da82de96dc6db4534d16`, `results/2026-08-12-sonnet-skill-screen.md`; records `8d6d671f9f8ea7cfc4dbe5069e8f36e764d3a19d`, `results/2026-08-12-sonnet-skill-screen/trials.jsonl` (187 rows). Its own caveat governs its use here: the 13–16 task spread it measured sits inside its ±1-task noise floor, so it means "no skill separated from control", and it explicitly does not license "skills don't help". It is consistent with arm C and arm F alike and discriminates neither.

## Next

Description determines whether a skill is read — 8/96, 14/96, 63/96 on one channel. If you ship a skill, the description decides whether the document exists at all for the agent. Three descriptions is enough to know the effect is large and not enough to predict a fourth.

Content determines whether reading it helps: +0.087 (p = 0.047) elective, +0.126 (p = 0.0069) forced, and only E − D survives multiplicity correction. Neither figure is clean. F − C may partly measure arm F's 642-byte frontmatter, which reaches all 96 contexts whether the document is opened or not; E − D is inflated by arm E's truncation, two of the four discriminating facts having sat in `references/` files arm E never received.

The elective channel costs about 0.06 relative to forcing the same document, and that estimate is marginal (p = 0.077). Do not treat it as established, and do not treat it as zero.

The unbiased-authoring question is still open and is the experiment worth running next. Both documents that helped were written by us with the failure set visible. Write a jj-0.44 reference from the docs and changelog alone, with no sight of which tasks fail, and run it in both arm D's slot and arm F's: that measures what a good-faith skill author gets, and running it in both re-estimates D − F on content nobody fitted.
